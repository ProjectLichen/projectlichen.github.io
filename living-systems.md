# Living Systems

Project Lichen begins with a simple recognition:

> **Life does not occur in isolation.**

Plants, fungi, microorganisms, insects, animals, water, soil, atmosphere and people participate in overlapping systems of exchange, reproduction, competition, cooperation and change.

A seed does not become a plant by itself.

Its possibilities depend upon soil, water, temperature, microorganisms, pollinators, genetics, sunlight and the actions of other living things. Those relationships in turn create new possibilities for organisms around them.

Project Lichen is interested in these relationships not simply as things to describe, but as **capabilities**.

What can a living system continue to do?

Can it reproduce?

Can it adapt?

Can it retain water?

Can it build soil?

Can it support other organisms?

Can it recover after disturbance?

And can its capabilities increase through the relationships occurring within it?

---

## Observation Before Prescription

Living systems are complex, local and continually changing.

For that reason, Project Lichen favors observation before prescription.

What works in one soil, climate or ecological community may behave very differently somewhere else.

Rather than assuming that there is a single ideal regenerative system, we can observe what actually happens:

**What arrived?**

**What survived?**

**What reproduced?**

**What interacted?**

**What changed?**

**What capabilities remain?**

Over time, repeated observations can become practical knowledge.

## From Seeds to Systems

Seeds provide one of Project Lichen's most tangible starting points.

A seed contains reproductive possibility, but that possibility is expressed through relationships with its surroundings.

From there the inquiry expands naturally:

**seed → plant → soil → water → pollinator → food → people → organic material → soil**

These are not separate subjects.

They are parts of interacting living systems.

Project Lichen explores how those relationships can be observed, documented and—where appropriate—strengthened.

---

## Living Systems in Practice

Much of Project Lichen grows from practical work with:

- seeds and seed saving
- food production
- soil building
- food forests
- pollinators and biodiversity
- fungi and decomposition
- water capture and retention
- perennial systems
- biological material cycles
- locally adapted plants
- observation across growing seasons

The intention is not simply to accumulate examples.

It is to ask what those examples can teach us about systems capable of continuing, adapting and reproducing.
