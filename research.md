# Research Library

> **Research becomes most useful when it changes what we are able to observe, ask or test.**

Project Lichen draws upon research from many fields.

Soils, plant biology, ecology, microbiology, agriculture, energy, computing, artificial intelligence, materials science, economics and complex systems may appear to be separate subjects.

Living systems rarely respect those boundaries.

The Research Library provides a place to preserve work that may help us understand the relationships among them.

---

## Why Keep a Research Library?

Project Lichen is grounded in observation and practical experience, but observation does not occur in isolation.

Other people are asking related questions.

Scientists are discovering previously unknown relationships.

Engineers are developing new ways of sensing, computing and using energy.

Growers are accumulating knowledge through practice.

Communities are experimenting with different ways of organizing and sharing resources.

Sometimes a piece of research explains something we have already observed.

Sometimes it challenges an assumption.

Sometimes it reveals a relationship we had not considered.

And sometimes its significance becomes apparent only much later.

For that reason, useful research is worth preserving even when we do not yet know exactly where it belongs.

---

## Evidence Over Certainty

Project Lichen values:

> **evidence over certainty and curiosity over conclusions**

A research paper is not treated as proof that an idea must be incorporated into Project Lichen.

Nor is research retained simply because it supports something we already believe.

Contradictory findings can be equally valuable.

The important questions are:

**What was actually observed?**

**How was it measured?**

**Under what conditions?**

**What remains uncertain?**

**Can the finding help us observe or test something ourselves?**

---

## Connecting Research to Practice

Project Lichen is particularly interested in research that crosses the boundary between explanation and capability.

For example:

A discovery about soil microorganisms may change how we understand soil carbon.

Research into plant sensing may suggest new ways of observing plant responses locally.

A computing architecture that reduces unnecessary data movement may change how we think about information, energy and Data Distance.

Research into seed genetics may help explain adaptation observed across growing seasons.

The subject areas differ.

The common question is:

> **What does this allow us to understand, observe or do that we could not before?**

## Research Across Boundaries

Areas of interest include:

- soil and microbial ecology
- seeds, genetics and plant adaptation
- biodiversity and pollinators
- food systems and regional foodsheds
- water and hydrology
- regenerative agriculture and horticulture
- climate and ecosystem resilience
- energy and material systems
- distributed and low-energy computing
- artificial intelligence
- complex adaptive systems
- economics and measures of value
- human coordination and governance

This list is intentionally open.

Project Lichen follows relationships rather than academic departments.

---

## From Research to Inquiry

Useful research may follow several paths through Project Lichen:

**research → question**

**research → observation**

**research → experiment**

**research → practical application**

**research → connection with another field**

or sometimes simply:

**research → remember this**

Not every useful discovery needs an immediate application.

Preserving it can leave that possibility available for later.

---

## An Evolving Library

This section will gradually collect selected papers, reports, projects and other substantive sources relevant to Project Lichen.

Where possible, entries should record not only **what the research says**, but also:

**Why did we retain it?**

**What Project Lichen question does it connect with?**

**What might we observe or test because of it?**

That context is important.

A collection of links tells us where information came from.

A research library should also help preserve **why we thought it mattered**.
