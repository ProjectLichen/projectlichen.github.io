# Field Notes

Project Lichen begins with observation.

Ideas about living systems become considerably more useful when they encounter actual soil, weather, seeds, insects, plants, failures, surprises and changing seasons.

Field Notes are where we record those encounters.

> **Observe carefully. Learn continually. Share generously.**

---

## Why Field Notes Matter

Living systems rarely behave exactly as expected.

The same seed may perform differently in different soils.

A planting that thrives one year may struggle the next.

An unexpected insect may become a pollinator, predator, pest—or all three under different circumstances.

Rainfall, temperature, timing, genetics and countless biological relationships influence what happens.

Rather than treating these variations simply as problems or experimental noise, Project Lichen treats them as information.

---

## Observation Before Conclusion

A Field Note does not need to prove anything.

It might record:

**What did we try?**

**What happened?**

**What did we expect to happen?**

**What surprised us?**

**What changed over time?**

**What questions emerged?**

And increasingly:

> **What capabilities remained, appeared, increased or disappeared?**

A useful observation may produce a better question rather than an immediate answer.

---

## Across Seasons and Generations

Some living processes can be observed in hours.

Others require seasons.

Still others become visible only across generations.

Seed saving provides a simple example.

A single harvest tells us something about yield.

Repeated planting and saving can begin to reveal information about adaptation, reproductive reliability, storage, disease, climate response and changing genetics.

The observation therefore does not necessarily end when the crop is harvested.

Sometimes that is where the next observation begins.

---

## Learning From Failure

Project Lichen does not intend Field Notes to become a collection only of successes.

Failed germination matters.

Crop loss matters.

Unexpected predation matters.

Poor soil matters.

Weather damage matters.

An experiment that produces the opposite of what we expected may teach us more than one that confirms our assumptions.

Recording failure also allows future experiments to begin with knowledge that would otherwise disappear.

---

## From Observation to Shared Knowledge

A single observation is local.

Repeated observations begin to form experience.

Shared observations allow comparison.

Over time, patterns may emerge that are useful beyond the place where the original observation occurred.

The aim is not to remove local differences, but to preserve enough context that others can decide whether an observation might be relevant where they are.

> **Every observation recorded today may become knowledge that benefits someone decades from now.**

---

## An Evolving Record

This section will gradually contain observations from Project Lichen's practical work with seeds, soils, food production, pollinators, biodiversity, water, material cycles and other living systems.

Some notes may be brief.

Some may develop into experiments or longer inquiries.

Some may simply preserve something interesting enough that it should not be forgotten.

That is sufficient reason to record it.
